# First-Principles Thinking — Professional

Professional work changes the constraint system itself: funding, ownership, policy, platform capability, or market assumptions.

Expose which constraints are physical, legal, economic, social, or self-imposed. Validate them with the responsible experts. Rebuild choices while accounting for path dependence, trust, reversibility, and the cost of coordination.

## Checklist

1. Define the outcome independent of the proposed mechanism.
2. Classify every constraint and cite its evidence or owner.
3. Identify constraints the organization can change.
4. Generate options from invariants.
5. Model transition and second-order effects.
6. Test the riskiest premise with limited blast radius.

```text
OUTCOME -> FACTS + INVARIANTS + VALUES -> OPTIONS -> TRANSITION -> EVIDENCE
              challenge inherited constraints without ignoring reality
```

## Test yourself

1. How would you challenge a company-wide “single cloud” rule?
2. Which constraints can engineering change, and which require executives or regulators?
3. How do you prevent first-principles thinking from becoming historical amnesia?
4. What evidence supports abandoning a sunk-cost platform?
