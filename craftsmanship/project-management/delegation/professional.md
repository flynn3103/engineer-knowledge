# Delegation — Professional

**Your question:** How do I scale leadership through delegation? How do I build an organization where people make good decisions independently, grow into leadership, and the organization doesn't depend on me?

Senior level teaches you to develop people through delegation. At professional level, you're building systems and culture that enable delegation at scale. You're not just delegating to one person; you're building an organization where delegation is how work gets done.

## Design and operations checklist

1. **Establish clear decision rights.** Who decides what? What requires escalation? What can be decided locally?
2. **Build shared mental models.** Everyone understands the strategy, constraints, and trade-offs. They can make decisions that align.
3. **Create feedback loops.** People see the results of their decisions quickly. They learn from mistakes and successes.
4. **Develop multiple leaders.** Don't build an organization that depends on you. Build one where multiple people can lead.
5. **Measure delegation health.** How often do people escalate? How often do they make good decisions independently? How often do they help others grow?

## Decision rights framework

Create a clear framework for who decides what:

| Decision | Who decides | Who is consulted | Who is informed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Technical architecture | Tech lead | Engineering team, product | All teams |
| Feature prioritization | Product manager | Engineering, design | All teams |
| Hiring | Manager | Team, HR | Department |
| Budget allocation | Finance | Department heads | All teams |
| On-call rotation | Team lead | Team members | Manager |

This framework prevents:
- Decisions being made without the right people
- Decisions being escalated unnecessarily
- People feeling left out of decisions that affect them

## Building shared mental models

People make good decisions when they understand:
- **The strategy:** Where are we going? Why?
- **The constraints:** What can't we change? What are the trade-offs?
- **The trade-offs:** What are we optimizing for? What are we sacrificing?
- **The values:** What matters to us? How do we make decisions?

Share these explicitly and often:
- In onboarding: "Here's how we think about trade-offs"
- In planning: "Here's the strategy for this quarter"
- In retrospectives: "Here's what we learned about our values"
- In 1-on-1s: "Here's how this decision aligns with our strategy"

## Developing multiple leaders

Don't build an organization that depends on you. Build one where multiple people can lead:

1. **Identify high-potential people.** Who has the skills and mindset to lead?
2. **Give them stretch assignments.** Let them lead projects, teams, or initiatives.
3. **Provide mentorship.** Help them navigate challenges and learn from mistakes.
4. **Celebrate their growth.** Make it clear that developing others is valued.
5. **Step back.** As they grow, give them more autonomy and responsibility.

## Realistic scenario: Scaling from 20 to 100 engineers

**Current state (20 engineers):**
- You make most decisions
- People come to you for guidance
- You know everyone and their work
- Decisions are fast but depend on you

**Problems:**
- You're a bottleneck
- People don't develop decision-making skills
- The organization can't scale
- You're burned out

**Target state (100 engineers):**
- Decisions are made at the right level
- People make good decisions independently
- Multiple people can lead
- You focus on strategy and culture

**Implementation plan:**

**Phase 1: Establish decision rights (Month 1-2)**
- Define who decides what
- Document the framework
- Train managers on the framework
- Start delegating decisions to managers

**Phase 2: Build shared mental models (Month 2-4)**
- Communicate strategy clearly
- Explain constraints and trade-offs
- Share decision-making rationale
- Create forums for discussion (all-hands, team meetings)

**Phase 3: Develop multiple leaders (Month 4-12)**
- Identify high-potential people
- Give them stretch assignments
- Provide mentorship
- Celebrate their growth

**Phase 4: Measure and improve (Ongoing)**
- Track decision-making patterns
- Gather feedback from teams
- Adjust the framework based on what's working
- Iterate on culture and values

## Feedback loops and learning

Create feedback loops so people learn from their decisions:

1. **Quick feedback:** For decisions with fast feedback (deployment, feature launch), review results within days.
2. **Regular retrospectives:** For longer-term decisions, review quarterly or after major milestones.
3. **Incident reviews:** When something goes wrong, review what happened and what to learn.
4. **Success reviews:** When something goes right, review what worked and why.

## Handling delegation at scale

As the organization grows, delegation becomes more complex:

**Coordination challenges:**
- Decisions made by one team affect other teams
- People don't know who to ask for decisions
- Decisions are made in silos without considering trade-offs

**Solutions:**
- Clear decision rights framework
- Regular cross-team communication
- Escalation paths for decisions that affect multiple teams
- Shared metrics and goals

**Culture challenges:**
- People are afraid to make decisions
- People escalate too much
- People make decisions without consulting others

**Solutions:**
- Psychological safety: make it safe to make decisions and fail
- Training: teach people how to make good decisions
- Feedback: give people feedback on their decisions
- Celebrate: celebrate good decisions and learning from mistakes

## Metrics and observability

Track delegation health:

| Metric | What it means | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Decision escalation rate | How often do people escalate decisions? | Low (most decisions made locally) |
| Decision quality | How often do decisions work out? | High (>80% of decisions are good) |
| Time to decision | How long does it take to make a decision? | Fast (most decisions within 1 week) |
| Leadership pipeline | How many people are ready for the next level? | High (multiple people per level) |
| Retention of high performers | Are people staying and growing? | High (>90% retention) |
