# Meeting — Junior

**Your question:** How do I run a meeting that actually accomplishes something?

Most meetings are a waste of time because they don't have a clear purpose, the wrong people are there, or there's no decision at the end. A good meeting has three things: a clear purpose, the right people, and a documented outcome.

## The method: purpose, people, and outcome

1. **Define the purpose in one sentence.** "Decide on the payment gateway for Q2" or "Align on the API design" or "Unblock the deployment." If you can't say it in one sentence, you don't have a clear purpose.

2. **Invite only the people who need to be there.** Not everyone who might be interested. People who need to:
   - Provide information (subject matter experts)
   - Make the decision (decision makers)
   - Implement the decision (implementers)
   - Be informed (stakeholders who need to know)

3. **Create an agenda.** List the topics, how long each will take, and what you want to accomplish for each topic.

4. **Run the meeting.** Stick to the agenda. If a topic goes over time, note it and move on.

5. **Document the outcome.** What was decided? Who is doing what? When is it due?

## A concrete example

**Bad meeting:**
"Let's sync on the API design. Everyone on the team is invited. We'll figure it out as we go."

Problems:
- No clear purpose (sync on what?)
- Too many people (everyone doesn't need to be there)
- No agenda (we'll figure it out as we go)
- No documented outcome (what did we decide?)

**Good meeting:**
"API Design Review — 30 minutes

Purpose: Decide on the authentication approach for the new API

Attendees:
- Sarah (API designer) — presents the options
- Mike (backend lead) — implementation perspective
- Lisa (security) — security review
- You (decision maker) — makes the final call

Agenda:
- (5 min) Sarah presents two options: JWT vs. OAuth
- (15 min) Discussion: trade-offs, implementation cost, security
- (5 min) Decision: which approach do we use?
- (5 min) Next steps: who implements, timeline

Outcome:
- Decision: OAuth for external APIs, JWT for internal services
- Sarah will document the decision and rationale
- Mike will estimate implementation effort
- Lisa will review the security implications
- Next review: Friday"

Better because:
- Clear purpose (decide on authentication approach)
- Right people (designer, implementer, security, decision maker)
- Clear agenda (what will we discuss, how long)
- Documented outcome (what was decided, who does what)

## Meeting types and their purposes

| Type | Purpose | Attendees | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| Decision meeting | Make a decision | Decision maker, experts, implementers | Decision documented, next steps clear |
| Alignment meeting | Build shared understanding | All affected teams | Everyone understands the plan |
| Brainstorm | Generate ideas | Diverse perspectives | Ideas documented, next steps clear |
| Retrospective | Learn from what happened | Team that did the work | Lessons learned, improvements identified |
| Status update | Share information | Stakeholders | Everyone knows the status |

## Common beginner mistakes

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| No clear purpose | People don't know why they're there | State the purpose in one sentence |
| Too many people | Meetings are slow, people feel unheard | Invite only people who need to be there |
| No agenda | Meetings go off track | Create an agenda before the meeting |
| Agenda items with no time | Meetings run over | Allocate time to each agenda item |
| No decision at the end | Nothing changes | End with a clear decision or next step |
| No documentation | People forget what was decided | Document the outcome and send it out |
| Attendees who don't participate | Wasted time | Invite people who will contribute |

## Hands-on checklist

Before you schedule a meeting, verify:

- [ ] Can you state the purpose in one sentence?
- [ ] Do you know who needs to be there?
- [ ] Have you created an agenda with time allocations?
- [ ] Do you know what decision or outcome you want?
- [ ] Will you document the outcome?
- [ ] Is this meeting necessary, or could it be an email?
