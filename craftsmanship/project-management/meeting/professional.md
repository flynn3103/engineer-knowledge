# Meeting — Professional

**Your question:** How do I build a meeting culture that scales? How do I reduce meeting load while increasing decision quality?

Senior level teaches you to use meetings strategically and know when not to meet. At professional level, you're building systems and culture that enable good decisions with minimal meetings. You're establishing norms, tools, and processes that scale across the organization.

## Design and operations checklist

1. **Establish meeting norms.** What types of meetings do we have? What's the purpose of each? When should we meet vs. decide asynchronously?
2. **Create decision-making processes.** How do we make decisions? Who decides what? How do we document decisions?
3. **Build asynchronous communication.** How do we share information and make decisions without meetings?
4. **Measure meeting health.** How many meetings do people attend? How much time is spent in meetings? Are decisions being made?
5. **Iterate on culture.** Gather feedback on meeting culture. What's working? What's not?

## Meeting types and norms

Define the types of meetings your organization has:

| Meeting Type | Purpose | Frequency | Attendees | Duration | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Decision meeting | Make a decision | As needed | Decision maker, experts | 30-60 min | Decision documented |
| Alignment meeting | Build shared understanding | Weekly/monthly | All affected teams | 30-60 min | Everyone understands the plan |
| Retrospective | Learn from what happened | After major milestones | Team that did the work | 60 min | Lessons learned, improvements |
| Status update | Share information | Weekly | Stakeholders | 15-30 min | Everyone knows the status |
| 1-on-1 | Individual development | Weekly | Manager, report | 30 min | Feedback, growth, blockers |
| All-hands | Company updates | Monthly | Everyone | 60 min | Everyone knows the direction |

## Asynchronous decision-making

Most decisions can be made asynchronously. Use this process:

1. **Write a document.** Describe the problem, options, trade-offs, and your recommendation.
2. **Share for feedback.** Give people time to read and comment (24-48 hours).
3. **Incorporate feedback.** Update the document based on comments.
4. **Make the decision.** Document the decision and reasoning.
5. **Communicate the decision.** Send it to everyone who needs to know.

**When to use async:**
- The decision is reversible
- There's no urgent deadline
- The decision doesn't require real-time discussion
- You have time to gather feedback

**When to meet:**
- The decision is irreversible or high-risk
- There's strong disagreement that needs real-time discussion
- The decision is urgent
- You need to build alignment across teams

## Building a meeting-light culture

At scale, meetings become a problem. Build a culture where:

1. **Meetings are optional.** If someone doesn't need to be there, they shouldn't be.
2. **Meetings are short.** 30 minutes is better than 1 hour. 15 minutes is better than 30.
3. **Meetings have a clear purpose.** If you can't state it in one sentence, don't meet.
4. **Meetings have a decision.** Every meeting should end with a clear decision or next step.
5. **Decisions are documented.** Every decision should be written down with reasoning.

## Realistic scenario: Scaling from 50 to 200 engineers

**Current state (50 engineers):**
- Lots of meetings (people spend 20+ hours/week in meetings)
- Decisions are made in meetings
- Information is shared in meetings
- People feel like they're in meetings all day

**Problems:**
- People don't have time to do work
- Decisions are slow
- Information is lost (people miss meetings)
- Meeting culture is toxic

**Target state (200 engineers):**
- Fewer meetings (people spend <10 hours/week in meetings)
- Decisions are made asynchronously
- Information is shared in documents
- Meetings are rare and valuable

**Implementation plan:**

**Phase 1: Establish meeting norms (Month 1-2)**
- Define meeting types and purposes
- Set expectations for meeting attendance
- Establish that meetings should be optional
- Create a "no meeting" time block (e.g., 9-11am for focus time)

**Phase 2: Build asynchronous decision-making (Month 2-4)**
- Create a decision-making template
- Train people on how to write decision documents
- Establish feedback and review process
- Document all decisions in a central location

**Phase 3: Reduce meeting load (Month 4-6)**
- Audit current meetings
- Cancel meetings that don't have a clear purpose
- Consolidate meetings where possible
- Move status updates to async (email, Slack, documents)

**Phase 4: Measure and improve (Ongoing)**
- Track meeting load (hours/week per person)
- Track decision quality (how often decisions work out)
- Gather feedback from teams
- Iterate on norms and processes

## Meeting audit

Periodically audit your meetings:

| Meeting | Purpose | Attendees | Frequency | Keep? | Change? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Weekly standup | Status update | 10 people | Weekly | No | Move to async |
| Architecture review | Make decisions | 5 people | As needed | Yes | Keep |
| All-hands | Company updates | 200 people | Monthly | Yes | Keep |
| 1-on-1 | Individual development | 2 people | Weekly | Yes | Keep |

For each meeting, ask:
- Is there a clear purpose?
- Are the right people there?
- Is this the best way to accomplish the purpose?
- Could this be async instead?

## Handling meeting culture problems

**Problem: Too many meetings**
- Audit meetings and cancel those without clear purpose
- Move status updates to async
- Create "no meeting" time blocks
- Measure meeting load and set targets

**Problem: Decisions are slow**
- Use async decision-making for reversible decisions
- Set clear decision deadlines
- Empower people to decide without consensus
- Document decisions clearly

**Problem: Information is lost**
- Document all decisions and information
- Create a central location for decisions (wiki, Confluence, etc.)
- Send summaries of meetings to people who didn't attend
- Use async communication (email, Slack) for important information

**Problem: Meeting culture is toxic**
- Make meetings optional
- Set expectations for respectful discussion
- Celebrate good decisions and learning from mistakes
- Measure meeting satisfaction and iterate

## Metrics and observability

Track meeting health:

| Metric | What it means | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Meeting hours/week per person | How much time do people spend in meetings? | <10 hours |
| Decision cycle time | How long does it take to make a decision? | <1 week for reversible, <2 weeks for irreversible |
| Decision quality | How often do decisions work out? | >80% |
| Meeting satisfaction | Do people feel meetings are valuable? | >4/5 |
| Async decision rate | What percentage of decisions are made async? | >70% |
