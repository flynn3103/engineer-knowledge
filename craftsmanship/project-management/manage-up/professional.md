# Manage Up — Professional

**Your question:** How do I establish norms for managing up? How do I develop managers? How do I create psychological safety?

Professional level teaches you to establish norms for managing up, develop managers, and create psychological safety. You're building a culture where people can manage up effectively and managers can lead effectively.

## Establishing norms for managing up

Establish what managing up means in your organization:

| Norm | What it means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Clear communication | Share progress, blockers, and needs regularly | Weekly updates, 1-on-1s |
| Honest feedback | Give and receive feedback constructively | "I appreciate the feedback. Here's what I'll do." |
| Escalation paths | Know when and how to escalate | "This is a prioritization decision. I need your help." |
| Psychological safety | Feel safe to raise issues and ask for help | "I'm stuck. Can you help me?" |
| Trust | Believe your manager has your back | Manager advocates for you |

## Creating psychological safety

Psychological safety means people feel safe to:
- Raise issues without fear of punishment
- Ask for help without fear of judgment
- Make mistakes without fear of blame
- Disagree without fear of retaliation

## A concrete example: Creating psychological safety

**Bad culture:**
- People hide problems
- People don't ask for help
- People blame each other
- People are afraid to disagree

**Good culture:**
- People raise issues early
- People ask for help
- People take responsibility
- People disagree respectfully

**How to create it:**

1. **Model it yourself.** Raise issues, ask for help, take responsibility, disagree respectfully.

2. **Reward it.** Celebrate when people raise issues early, ask for help, take responsibility.

3. **Punish the opposite.** Address when people hide problems, blame others, or retaliate.

4. **Teach it.** Help people learn how to manage up effectively.

## Developing managers

Help your managers become better at managing up:

1. **Teach them what managing up means.** Share frameworks and examples.

2. **Give them feedback.** Help them see how they're doing.

3. **Model it.** Show them how to manage up effectively.

4. **Reflect with them.** Help them learn from their experiences.

## A concrete example: Developing a manager

**Situation:** Your manager is not listening to your team's concerns about workload.

**Bad approach:**
"My manager is bad at managing. There's nothing I can do."

Problems:
- You're not helping them improve
- The situation doesn't change

**Good approach:**

You: "I want to help you be a better manager. I've noticed that when the team raises concerns about workload, you don't seem to listen. I think you care, but maybe you're not hearing us.

Can we talk about this? I'd like to help you understand what we're experiencing."

Manager: "OK, I'm listening."

You: "Here's what's happening:
- We're 2x overloaded
- We have no time for code review or testing
- People are burning out
- We've raised this multiple times, but nothing changes

I think you might not realize how serious this is. Can we work together to solve it?"

Manager: "I didn't realize it was that bad. What can I do?"

You: "Here are some options:
1. Reduce feature scope
2. Hire more engineers
3. Reduce infrastructure maintenance

I recommend option 1 or 2. Can you talk to the product team about reducing scope?"

Manager: "OK, I'll do that."

Better because:
- You helped your manager understand the issue
- You proposed solutions
- You worked together to solve it

## Measuring managing up health

Track whether managing up is working:

| Metric | What it tells you | Healthy signal |
|---|---|---|
| Issue escalation | Are issues being raised early? | Issues are raised within 1 day of discovery |
| Feedback quality | Are people giving and receiving feedback? | >80% of people receive feedback regularly |
| Psychological safety | Do people feel safe to raise issues? | >80% of people feel safe to raise issues |
| Manager effectiveness | Are managers helping their teams? | >80% of people trust their manager |
| Retention | Are people staying? | <10% annual turnover |

## Realistic scenario: Building a managing up culture

**Current state (50 engineers, 5 teams):**
- People hide problems
- People don't ask for help
- Managers don't listen
- Psychological safety is low
- Turnover is high (20%)

**Target state (100 engineers, 10 teams):**
- People raise issues early
- People ask for help
- Managers listen and help
- Psychological safety is high
- Turnover is low (5%)

**Implementation plan:**

### Phase 1: Establish norms (Month 1)

**Define managing up norms:**
- Clear communication
- Honest feedback
- Escalation paths
- Psychological safety
- Trust

**Share with the organization:**
- Explain what managing up means
- Give examples
- Get feedback

### Phase 2: Teach skills (Month 2-3)

**Teach how to communicate progress:**
- What to share
- When to share
- How to share

**Teach how to raise issues:**
- How to identify issues
- How to explain impact
- How to propose solutions

**Teach how to ask for help:**
- How to be specific
- How to respect time
- How to follow up

**Teach how to handle feedback:**
- How to listen
- How to ask questions
- How to take action

### Phase 3: Develop managers (Month 3-6)

**Teach managers how to listen:**
- How to create psychological safety
- How to ask good questions
- How to help their teams

**Teach managers how to escalate:**
- When to escalate
- How to escalate
- How to follow up

**Teach managers how to give feedback:**
- How to be specific
- How to be constructive
- How to follow up

### Phase 4: Measure and improve (Ongoing)

**Track managing up metrics:**
- Issue escalation
- Feedback quality
- Psychological safety
- Manager effectiveness
- Retention

**Gather feedback:**
- How is managing up working?
- What's hard?
- What could be better?

**Iterate:**
- Adjust norms based on feedback
- Improve training
- Improve processes

## Common mistakes at professional level

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| No clear norms | People manage up differently | Establish clear norms |
| Not modeling it | People don't believe the norms | Model managing up yourself |
| Not teaching skills | People don't know how to manage up | Teach managing up skills |
| Not developing managers | Managers don't know how to listen | Develop managers |
| Not measuring | You don't know if it's working | Track managing up metrics |
