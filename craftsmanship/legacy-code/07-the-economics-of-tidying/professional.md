# The Economics of Tidying — Professional

At organization scale, tidying competes with feature delivery for shared capacity. Make its cost, payoff, ownership, and limit visible so it is funded as a bounded investment rather than defended as a virtue.

## Make the case in delivery terms

Translate “messy code” into evidence: recurring estimate overruns, incident frequency, slow reviews, high onboarding time, or a known sequence of roadmap changes. State the scope, cost cap, expected payback, and what will make you stop.

## Funding patterns

- Include small tidyings in the feature work that benefits immediately.
- Fund hotspot reductions with explicit milestones and operational metrics.
- Reserve large modernization for boundaries with measurable business or reliability risk.
- Avoid open-ended cleanup sprints with no consumer, metric, or exit criteria.

## Governance without bureaucracy

Maintain a visible hotspot list with owners and evidence. Review investments periodically against actual cycle time, defect rate, and delivery forecasts. Retire work that no longer has a credible payoff, even if it is already partially complete.

## A healthy outcome

Teams can explain why they are tidying, keep feature delivery predictable, and demonstrate that repeated work in important areas is becoming cheaper and safer over time.
