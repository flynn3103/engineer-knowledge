# Incident Management — Professional

<!-- level-focus -->
At professional level, focus on this question:

> Can you create an incident operating model that coordinates sustained delivery, external obligations, and learning across an organization?

---

## Design the system around people

Set incident levels, delegated authority, executive communication, legal/security involvement, and handoff rules before an emergency. Maintain a trained command rotation rather than relying on a few heroes. Team runbooks should expose dependencies and safe actions without requiring global knowledge.

## Sustained scenario

A multi-day vendor outage affects several products. Establish a central command for customer policy and vendor escalation, while product cells own feature degradation. Daily decisions are evidence-based: impact by cohort, capacity, recovery probability, and regulatory commitments. Exit only when normal service, backlog reconciliation, and communications obligations are complete.

## Outcomes

Measure mitigation time, customer-impact duration, responder load, handoff quality, and follow-up completion—not merely incident count. Governance should fund recurring systemic improvements while protecting teams from performative reporting.

## Apply it

1. Define authority for product, central command, and executives.
2. Plan a multi-day outage cadence and handoff.
3. Set three outcome measures and an improvement review.

## Verify your work

- Command continues safely across shift changes.
- External commitments are represented in incident decisions.
- Improvements are funded against demonstrated failure patterns.

## Review questions

- Why is a command rotation more reliable than a hero model?
- What expands an incident from technical response to organizational response?
- Which metrics expose responder sustainability?
