# Postmortems — Professional

<!-- level-focus -->
At professional level, focus on this question:

> Can you run a learning system that converts incident evidence into durable organizational investment and accountability?

---

## Learning portfolio

Use a common, blameless record format but allow teams to own analysis. Aggregate themes quarterly and fund controls that reduce the most consequential exposure. Protect psychological safety while maintaining accountability for action completion, risk acceptance, and customer commitments.

## Sustained scenario

Postmortems reveal repeated restoration failures across products. Create a cross-team restore program: standard backup contracts, quarterly evidence-producing drills, and exception review for systems unable to meet recovery targets. Roll out by criticality; do not halt every product for a uniform migration.

## Exit conditions

Program success is demonstrated restore evidence, reduced recovery uncertainty, and closed high-risk gaps—not the number of documents written. Escalate overdue risk to accountable leadership with options: fund, reduce scope, or explicitly accept it.

## Apply it

1. Define a quarterly theme-review process.
2. Decompose one systemic fix into reversible adoption increments.
3. Define evidence-based exit conditions.

## Verify your work

- Themes lead to funded controls rather than generic reminders.
- Exceptions have owners and review dates.
- Drills produce evidence that recovery promises are real.

## Review questions

- What distinguishes a learning portfolio from a document archive?
- How should leaders handle unfunded reliability risk?
- Why are drills stronger evidence than plans?
