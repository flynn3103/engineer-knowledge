# Toil Reduction — Professional

<!-- level-focus -->
At professional level, focus on this question:

> Can you build a sustainable automation program that improves team capacity while governing risk and ownership?

---

## Portfolio, not scripts

Measure toil by interruption frequency, operator time, and growth rate. Fund high-leverage workflows through a shared automation platform with audited execution, policy review, and local product ownership. Preserve cognitive load: teams should operate a small number of consistent tools, not dozens of bespoke bots.

## Scenario

Five teams each manually remediate common queue failures. Deliver a shared executor first, then migrate one policy at a time with shadow mode, regional rollout, and rollback. Product teams retain policy ownership; platform owns executor availability and safety controls. Exit when operator time drops and exception quality improves.

## Governance

Review authority expansion, failed actions, override use, and capacity returned to planned work. Require threat modeling or compliance review when automation changes access, data, or production capacity. Escalate automation risk like any other production change.

## Apply it

1. Rank an automation portfolio by time saved and blast radius.
2. Define shared-platform and local-policy ownership.
3. Set outcome measures and a governance review.

## Verify your work

- Capacity gains are measured in reduced interruptions, not script count.
- Automation authority is auditable and periodically reviewed.
- Teams can safely stop or roll back migrated policies.

## Review questions

- Why is a shared executor preferable to repeated bespoke scripts?
- Which automation metrics indicate hidden risk?
- How can automation reduce cognitive load?
